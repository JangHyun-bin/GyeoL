# Writing System

Gyeol treats Korean and English as native sibling outputs.

## Korean

- Write in natural Korean business prose, not translated English order.
- Prefer clear subject and action over decorative phrasing.
- Keep metrics close to the sentence that explains them.
- Avoid overusing slash pairs and parenthetical English.
- For headings, use short noun phrases or direct promises.
- For body copy, preserve Korean rhythm with moderate sentence length.

## English

- Use concise business prose.
- Prefer specific claims over broad adjectives.
- Keep section rhythm predictable: claim, evidence, implication.
- Avoid inflated startup language unless source material requires it.

## Bilingual Work

- Do not translate mechanically.
- Keep the information architecture aligned.
- Let examples, idioms, and sentence rhythm differ by language.

## Investment / Strategy Quality Bar

Investment and strategy documents must connect technology, market, economics, and risk in the same information architecture.

Required elements:

- company context and stage
- thesis or decision
- metrics with units
- market wedge
- technology moat
- go-to-market path
- risks and mitigations
- source or sample-data notice

Korean output must sound like Korean business writing. English output must sound like native investor prose. Do not translate headings mechanically.
